# Philosophy

Two beliefs sit underneath most of what I do: that things should be open, and
that we should build people up. They are really the same belief looked at from
two angles.

## Things should be open

Knowledge is not diminished by being shared. If I teach you something, I do not
have less of it — we both have it now, and you can carry it somewhere I never
could. That is the strange and wonderful thing about knowledge, and it is why
hoarding it has always struck me as a kind of self-inflicted poverty. A locked
door helps no one on either side of it.

So my default is open. Open code, open notes, open reasoning. When I work
something out, I would rather show the working than guard the answer. Not
because I owe anyone an explanation, but because the explanation is the most
useful part. The answer helps one person once. The understanding helps
everyone, forever.

Open does not mean coerced. I do not think you force people to share by
demanding it; you invite them by going first, by making generosity the obvious
and easy thing. You lead with the door already open and trust that most people,
shown the way, will hold it open for the next person too.

## We should build people up

The reason openness matters is people. Information set free is only worth
something if it lands in someone and makes them more capable, more confident,
more able to make the thing they were afraid they couldn't.

I would rather spend my effort lifting someone than proving I stand above them.
Gatekeeping — making things deliberately hard, obscure, or exclusive so that
knowing feels like status — is the thing I want least to be. Every time I
explain instead of dismiss, encourage instead of belittle, hand someone the
tool instead of performing the trick, the whole room gets a little stronger.
None of that costs me anything I actually wanted to keep.

Building people up is not flattery and it is not lowering the bar. Sometimes it
is honest, even hard, feedback — given because I believe the person can meet
it. The point is the belief underneath: that people are worth investing in, and
that helping someone become more than they were is one of the few things that
reliably makes the world better.

## The same belief, twice

Open the doors, and lift the people walking through them. Share what you know,
and use it to make others stronger rather than to keep yourself ahead. I do not
always live up to it, but it is the direction I try to point in — and it is the
direction I would want anything I make to point in too.
